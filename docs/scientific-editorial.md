# Scientific editorial

Scientific editorial is a derived `chao-ui` profile for posters, covers, campaign imagery, research landing pages, and visual assets that should feel rigorous, unfamiliar, and consequential. Read [experience principles](experience-principles.md) first. This profile places emotional spectacle inside an exact editorial system.

The work should feel like an unknown, physically present phenomenon documented by a future research institution. Four tensions define the family:

- emotional wonder against rational measurement
- monumental scale against microscopic notation
- tactile matter against abstract geometry
- active space against one concentrated revelation

Bright Field and Dark Field are two presentations of this shared system. Choose one primary field for each composition.

## Preserve the subject

- Preserve the requested subject, meaning, format, and exact copy.
- Translate the subject through physical behavior and visual relationships. Do not replace it with circles, rocks, lattices, or space imagery merely because those forms appear in this guide.
- Extract contrast, scale, density, placement, and light behavior from references. Do not reproduce their names, logos, layouts, wording, or distinctive objects without explicit authorization.
- Treat this document as art direction. It is not a checklist of objects that every image must contain.

## How the profile instantiates the general model

| General dimension | Scientific-editorial choice |
| --- | --- |
| Context | research campaign, poster, cover, or editorial page |
| Perceptual fluency | strong hierarchy, sparse grouping, decisive contrast |
| Familiarity | institutional typography, captions, rules, and measurement cues |
| Novelty | an unfamiliar physical phenomenon or conceptual transfer |
| Complexity | a restrained annotation layer around a dominant subject |
| Ambiguity | enough openness to invite inquiry, with recoverable structural clues |
| Meaning path | observation, measurement, partial system, and supplied context |
| Affect | wonder, curiosity, gravity, or controlled unease |
| Evidence | real copy, data, equations, labels, and provenance remain native and exact |
| Time course | immediate hierarchy followed by slower inspection and interpretation |

Darkness, pale mineral fields, monoliths, orbital forms, and lattices belong to this profile. Future `chao-ui` profiles may choose entirely different materials, palettes, metaphors, and emotional registers while following the same general experience model.

## Profile invariants

1. **One visual sovereign.** One phenomenon, object, structure, or field leads the composition. Supporting marks remain subordinate.
2. **Active space dominates.** Bright Field uses warm pale space. Dark Field uses coal or blue-black space. The field creates scale and silence rather than serving as empty fill.
3. **One event reveals structure.** Concentrate attention at a seam, node, aperture, contour, trajectory, intersection, shadow, inclusion, or change in material behavior.
4. **Spectacle is measured.** Sparse rules, axes, ticks, nodes, guides, or supplied metadata impose order without becoming a decorative interface.
5. **Scale contrast remains visible.** Pair a monumental crop with hairline graphics, small marks, distant particles, or restrained metadata.
6. **Material remains credible.** Grain, fiber, dust, roughness, vapor, translucency, structural thickness, cast shadow, and optical falloff provide physical evidence.
7. **Color remains restrained.** Neutral fields and one reveal color dominate. Accent color occupies a minor, meaningful role.
8. **The frame implies a larger program.** Cropping, partial diagrams, observation numbers, edge marks, or interrupted structures suggest one plate inside a broader body of research.

## Bright Field

Bright Field presents an unfamiliar specimen under abundant, exact light. Mystery comes from form, scale, and material behavior.

### Active field

- Warm paper white, pale mineral, chalk, or porcelain space should occupy roughly 60–80% of the frame.
- Prefer off-white depth to pure flat digital white when physical presence would help.
- Use asymmetric placement and decisive cropping. A pale subject may cross the frame or become visible through its boundary, relief, or shadow.

### Revelation

- Anchor the image with one dense event such as a graphite aperture, ink-dark seam, concentrated shadow, amber inclusion, or mineral-green node.
- Reveal structure through grazing light, embossing, translucency, cut edges, absorption, or cast shadow.
- Keep edges controlled. Low contrast should create restraint without making the subject illegible.

### Palette and material

- Field: warm paper `#f3f0e7`
- Surface: pale mineral `#e8e5da`
- Primary ink: `#171a18`
- Secondary graphite: `#666960`
- Rules: `#c9c6ba`
- Accent: desaturated olive `#666b4e`
- Optional concentrated inclusion: muted amber `#a77c45`
- Materials: uncoated paper, vellum, ceramic, chalk, pale stone, frosted glass, fiber, translucent membrane, or structural relief

Avoid sterile medical brochures, luxury skincare photography, beige lifestyle minimalism, translucent blue biotech blobs, soft background gradients, and frictionless white plastic renders.

## Dark Field

Dark Field presents an unfamiliar phenomenon in deep negative space. One warm emission reveals the structural event.

### Active field

- Coal-black, blue-black, black-green, or very dark navy space should occupy roughly 55–75% of the frame or remain perceptually dominant when the subject is large.
- Let the dominant form cross or exceed the frame rather than floating as a centered icon.
- Preserve darkness and silence before adding particulate detail or graphic marks.

### Revelation

- Warm bone-white or ivory emission should appear at one contour, seam, node, aperture, trajectory, or intersection.
- Keep bloom localized and retain readable edges around the bright event.
- Let light fall rapidly into darkness. Atmosphere may catch the light but should not fill the frame with fog.

### Palette and material

- Field: near-black `#080c0f`
- Surface: black-green `#11171a`
- Primary ink: bone white `#f1ead8`
- Secondary neutral: smoke gray `#99998f`
- Rules: graphite `#353b38`
- Reveal: warm ivory `#e9d7a3`
- Accent: desaturated olive `#858b68`
- Materials: rough mineral, folded mass, membrane, architectural fragment, dust, vapor, particulate matter, or open structure with visible thickness

Avoid cyan-magenta neon, rainbow color, glowing code, cyberpunk skylines, holographic dashboards, broad bloom, fantasy nebulae, and glossy black plastic.

## Visual modes

Choose the mode from the subject's underlying logic. A secondary mode may support the composition, but the modes should not compete.

| Subject logic | Mode | Bright Field expression | Dark Field expression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| relation, causality, mathematics, protocols | Measured geometry | embossed arcs, incised trajectories, graphite intersections | luminous arcs, plotted nodes, warm intersections |
| rupture, emergence, hidden force, transformation | Specimen or monolith | ceramic, folded-paper, or pale mineral mass with one cavity or inclusion | rough mass, membrane, or fragment with one emitting seam |
| continuity, recurrence, time, memory, gravity | Orbital or interference field | faint rings, pressure patterns, translucent wavefronts | ring, aperture, halo, wavefront, or field disturbance |
| growth, infrastructure, computation, collective structure | Generative lattice | ivory frameworks with directional shadows and select dense junctions | open trusses or filaments with light concentrated at select nodes |

Measured geometry should remain analytic rather than becoming generic sacred geometry. Monoliths should avoid familiar science-fiction silhouettes. Orbital fields should avoid generic planets and galaxies. Lattices should avoid repetitive wallpaper and fully illuminated CAD renders.

## Composition and density

- Prefer portrait orientation for posters and covers. Adapt to the destination when another ratio is required.
- Use asymmetric balance, offset focal placement, decisive crops, and one quiet copy zone.
- A useful hierarchy is dominant phenomenon, localized revelation, title or copy zone, sparse measurement marks, then atmospheric residue.
- **Low density:** one phenomenon, minimal marks, and a large quiet field.
- **Medium density:** one subject, a restrained diagram layer, a few nodes or particles, and a clear copy-safe area. This is the default.
- **High density:** a complex lattice or technical construction with one dominant direction and one hierarchy of revelation.

Higher density does not permit additional focal subjects, colors, or interface decoration.

## Graphic and typographic system

Graphic marks should feel functional, thin, and quiet:

- hairline grids, construction arcs, ruled baselines, corner crop marks, supplied coordinates, and sparse nodes
- low-contrast graphite for secondary structure and the field's reveal color for one important path or intersection
- incomplete or cropped systems rather than a decorative full-screen interface

Typography uses:

- IBM Plex Sans for large display titles, controlled title case, and readable copy
- Inconsolata for supplied metadata, observation numbers, coordinates, labels, and captions
- strong scale contrast between the title and metadata
- clean alignment to an editorial grid
- limited copy and ample air

Reusable generated assets should normally contain no text. Place exact copy with native HTML, CSS, SVG, or the final layout tool. When text must appear inside an image, quote it verbatim, specify its placement and hierarchy, and allow no additional generated text. Use ticks, ruled lines, or blank label fields instead of fake microtext.

## Generation brief

Use this structure when directing an image generator or another designer:

```text
Asset and format: intended use, aspect ratio, visual-only or designed layout
Subject: the user's concept expressed as one dominant unknown phenomenon
Field: Bright Field or Dark Field
Primary visual mode: measured geometry, specimen or monolith, orbital field, or generative lattice
Composition: asymmetric placement, decisive crop, active field, and intentional copy-safe area
Revelation: one localized seam, node, aperture, shadow, inclusion, trajectory, or intersection
Light and material: field-appropriate light behavior with tactile physical evidence
Graphic system: sparse functional rules, coordinates, ticks, or supplied nodes
Palette: the selected field palette with one minor accent
Typography: only exact requested copy, otherwise visual-only
Constraints: preserve the subject, one focal phenomenon, no decorative overload, no invented evidence
```

Describe relationships explicitly. The field dominates detail. The subject dominates annotations. One concentrated event anchors quieter marks. Physical mystery remains disciplined by editorial order.

## Scientific and functional integrity

- Generate real plots, equations, measurements, labels, and diagrams from authoritative data or code.
- Use generated imagery for atmosphere, material, scale, and the conceptual key visual.
- Give informative imagery useful alternative text. Use empty alternative text for decoration that adds no information.
- Keep exact copy selectable and responsive when the destination supports native text.
- Preserve readable contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, reading order, and interaction states in every surrounding interface.
- Keep navigation and controls on standard `chao-ui` surfaces. Editorial profiles provide the canvas around content rather than changing control behavior.

## HTML integration

Import `theme.css` before `editorial.css`, then select a field on the contained canvas:

```html
<section class="pf-editorial" data-field="bright">
  <div class="pf-editorial-layout">
    <div class="pf-editorial-visual">
      <img src="key-visual.webp" alt="Description of the observed phenomenon">
    </div>
    <div class="pf-editorial-copy">
      <div class="pf-editorial-heading">
        <p class="pf-editorial-eyebrow">Research programme 04</p>
        <h1 class="pf-editorial-title">Collective structure</h1>
        <hr class="pf-editorial-rule">
        <p class="pf-editorial-deck">Exact project copy belongs in native text.</p>
      </div>
      <dl class="pf-editorial-metadata">
        <div><dt>Field</dt><dd>Bright</dd></div>
        <div><dt>Mode</dt><dd>Generative lattice</dd></div>
      </dl>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
```

Use `data-field="dark"` for Dark Field. Product CSS may control the destination-specific width, ratio, placement, and surrounding document layout.

## Evaluation rubric

Score each item 0, 1, or 2. A strong result scores at least 13 out of 16 and has no zero among the first four items.

1. **Subject fidelity:** the requested idea remains recognizable.
2. **Single hierarchy:** one dominant phenomenon leads the composition.
3. **Active-field dominance:** the chosen pale or dark field creates scale and restraint.
4. **Localized revelation:** one structural event concentrates attention.
5. **Editorial order:** sparse graphics and typography feel functional and intentional.
6. **Material credibility:** surfaces, particles, atmosphere, relief, or optical behavior feel physical.
7. **Palette discipline:** field neutrals dominate and accent color remains minor.
8. **Original transfer:** the result belongs to the system without copying a reference object, wording, or layout.

Revise the lowest-scoring criterion first. Change one variable at a time and preserve criteria already scoring 2. Make no more than two correction passes without returning to the requester for direction.
