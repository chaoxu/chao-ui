Weighted Norms and a Polygon Example

1 Comparing Two Norms

For a positive integer $n$ and a vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the $\ell_1$ norm adds the absolute values of its coordinates. The Euclidean norm takes the square root of the sum of their squares.1

Theorem1Norm Inequality

Every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfies $\lVert x\rVert_2 \le \lVert x\rVert_1$. Equality holds exactly when at most one coordinate is nonzero.

Proof

Expanding the square gives

$$\begin{aligned} \lVert x\rVert_1^2 &= \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 + 2\sum_{i<j}|x_i x_j|\\ &\ge \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 = \lVert x\rVert_2^2. \end{aligned}$$

Taking nonnegative square roots proves the inequality. Equality holds exactly when every product $x_i x_j$ with $i \ne j$ is zero.

2 A Weighted Norm on a Polygon

The diagram uses a polygon $P$ inside the box $[-2.5,1.5]\times[-1.65,1.65]$. Its additional constraints are

$$\begin{aligned} x_1 + 0.32x_2 &\le -0.95,\\ 0.78x_1 - 0.52x_2 &\le -0.95,\\ |0.18x_1 + x_2| &\le 0.62. \end{aligned}$$

For positive weights $w_1,w_2$, the set $\{x:w_1|x_1|+w_2|x_2|\le r\}$ is a diamond when $r>0$. The animation grows this diamond until it first touches $P$. The contact point minimizes the weighted norm over this polygon.2

例1向量 $(3,4)$ 的范数

取 $x=(3,4)$，则 $\lVert x\rVert_1=|3|+|4|=7$，$\lVert x\rVert_2=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$。两个坐标均非零，所以这里的不等式为严格不等式 $5<7$。
